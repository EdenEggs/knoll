· RULE THE SIXTH · ON FOREVER ·
K
eeps
i.
What the hill votes in, the hill keeps. To take it back, ask the hill. It listens, slowly.
ii.
Nothing is un-kept in a huff. Sleep on it. The hill will still be here in the morning.
iii.
Old is not worse. The first doodle on the hill outranks the newest, on principle.
iv.
The hill belongs to everyone, which is to say to you, which is to say: mind it.

· RULE THE FIFTH · ON TIDYING ·
W
ardens
i.
Wardens may pin, unpin, and sweep. They must say why, in words, where the hill can read them.
ii.
Wardens are chosen by the hill and can be unchosen by it. Rakes are returned at the gate.
iii.
Disagree with a sweep? Petition the hill. The hill outranks the rake.
iv.
Wardens are volunteers. Thank one sometime, and watch them not know what to do with it.

“forever, here, means until
the hill says otherwise.”

· RULE THE FOURTH · ON CREDIT ·
S
ignatures
i.
Made it? Sign it. Found it? Say where. Not sure? Then not yet.
ii.
Remixes are welcome. Remixes in a false moustache are not. Name the original.
iii.
Claim another’s work as your own and you forfeit your hat. Hats are how we tell you apart.
iv.
The people who made the thing we love are neighbours too. Treat their work like a borrowed ladder: gladly, carefully, and give it back.
IV
“the wardens carry rakes,
not gavels.”
iv
· RULE THE THIRD · ON VOTING ·
B
allots
i.
One gnome, one vote. A second account is not a second gnome. It is a costume, and we can tell.
ii.
Vote for what the hill should keep, not for whoever asked nicest.
iii.
No marching in a crowd from elsewhere. The hill can feel a stampede coming.
iv.
You may vote for your own. Everyone does. Just don’t act surprised when it wins.
III
“every mark has a hand behind it.
name the hand.”

· RULE THE SECOND · ON OFFERINGS ·
P
inning
i.
Pin only what belongs to this hill’s subject. Other subjects get their own hills, eventually.
ii.
Keep it fit for all ages. If you have to ask, it isn’t.
iii.
Post it once. Six copies is not six votes; it is one very tired hill.
iv.
Unfinished is welcome, so long as it says so. Pretending is not.
II
“nothing here was placed.
everything here was voted.”

· RULE THE FIRST · ON MANNERS ·
N
eighbours
i.
Critique the work, never the hands that made it. The work can take it.
ii.
No slurs, no threats, no dogpiles. The hill is small and sound carries.
iii.
Newcomers get the benefit of the doubt. You had it once too; you just don’t remember.
iv.
If you must be a pain, be a brief one.
I
“a hill without rules
is just a pile.”
