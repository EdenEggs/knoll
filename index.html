A place where EVERY fan leaves a mark

Doodle, stamp shapes, leave notes, queue up the jukebox, or beat someone's snake high score. The best of it gets voted onto the page for good.

Sketch · not built yet The petition board

Anyone can put a daft question to the hill. The part that matters is the last step: when yes wins it isn't a poll result, it's a pond. Here's the whole loop, on repeat.

Sketch · not built yet The workshop shed

The roadmap, except it is a shed. Whatever is being built sits on the workbench as a half-finished contraption and looks more finished as it gets closer; anything further out is a lump under a tarp with a question mark on it. When a thing ships, the gnome carries it out and screws it to the hill.

More of the rulebook
Sketch

The museum

Each cycle the top-voted drawing is preserved — framed, varnished, hung on a permanent gallery wall. Everything left unpreserved fades out or gets built over.

Sketch

The naming ceremony

When a new landmark turns up, the hill votes on what it is called. Names stick forever and show on hover. The pond, for the record, is called Greg.

Sketch

Elections

Once a month the hill elects a mayor gnome from whoever stands. The winner wanders the page in a tiny sash for their term. Zero power, maximum lore.

Sketch

The law book

Every petition that passes is written into a slightly absurd constitution. New visitors can read the hill’s whole legislative history, which doubles as a changelog.

Contribution, not votes
Sketch

The monument

A build too big for one person: it only advances when enough people each place a single block. The progress bar is the scaffolding. Finish it and the builders go on a plaque.

Sketch

The time capsule

A buried chest that opens on a fixed date. Anyone can put one drawing or note in before it seals, and nobody sees inside until the day it spills back onto the hill.
